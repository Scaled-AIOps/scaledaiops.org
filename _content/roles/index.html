Roles

Who directs, verifies, governs, runs and measures AI-assisted work, and which existing roles they grow out of.

Roles are responsibilities, not necessarily new job titles or new headcount. Each one evolves from a role organisations already have and adds the accountability AI adoption demands.

A small organisation combines several roles in one person; a large one staffs them separately, often across teams. Either way, every role has a named, accountable human: AI can support the work of any role, but it never holds one.

Engineering Orchestrator

Evolves from: senior / lead engineer

Mission: direct AI through every stage of the lifecycle and own each resulting change, its intent and its correctness.

Responsibilities

  • Break work into specified, tiered tasks before any generation starts
  • Choose what to delegate to AI within the limits set by the change's risk tier
  • Review generated code, tests and infrastructure-as-code against the agreed specification
  • Mark AI involvement on every change and answer for it in review and incidents
  • Feed back failures and rework into delegation limits and context assets

Key artefacts: approved specs and acceptance criteria, changes with provenance trailers, ADRs recording AI involvement, review records.

Disciplines: AI-Augmented Delivery · Human–AI Workflow Design

Eval Engineer

Evolves from: QA / test engineer

Mission: make AI output verifiable by building the evals and checks that decide whether generated work, prompts and agents are fit to ship.

Responsibilities

  • Design eval suites for prompts, agents and AI features, with pass thresholds per risk tier
  • Wire evals and test generation into the verify stage of the pipeline
  • Re-run evals when a prompt, context pack, tool or AI service version changes
  • Assemble evidence bundles for high- and critical-tier releases
  • Track escaped defects in AI-assisted work and turn them into new checks

Key artefacts: eval suites and datasets, pass thresholds per tier, eval run records, evidence bundles.

Disciplines: AI-Augmented Delivery · Governance & Guardrails

Context Engineer

Evolves from: technical writer, developer-experience engineer

Mission: curate the prompts, skills and context packs that AI works from, and manage them as versioned, reviewed and owned assets.

Responsibilities

  • Keep prompts, skills and context packs in version control with an owner and a review path
  • Write project context (conventions, architecture notes, domain terms) that AI tools can consume
  • Keep sensitive data out of context and apply context hygiene checks
  • Retire stale or duplicated context assets and record which changes used which version
  • Work with Eval Engineers so every material context change is evaluated before release

Key artefacts: context packs, prompt and skill libraries, context changelogs, context hygiene rules.

Disciplines: Tooling & Context Engineering

AI Workflow Designer

Evolves from: business analyst, process engineer

Mission: design how work moves between people and AI, including what is delegated, where approval gates sit and when a human takes over.

Responsibilities

  • Map each workflow step to a delegation level: auto-approve, human-review or human-only
  • Place approval gates according to risk tier criteria, not convenience
  • Define handoffs between agents and people, and the escalation path when AI is uncertain or fails
  • Set agent autonomy levels and the action limits that bound them
  • Revise delegation from evidence: incidents, rework and review burden

Key artefacts: delegation maps, approval-gate definitions, handoff and escalation runbooks, agent autonomy profiles.

Disciplines: Human–AI Workflow Design · Governance & Guardrails

AI Adoption Lead

Evolves from: agile coach, delivery manager

Mission: run the adoption programme so that teams take up AI in small, reversible steps, and base each expansion on evidence.

Responsibilities

  • Run maturity self-assessments and turn the results into an adoption roadmap
  • Plan and run enablement, including AI literacy for everyone who uses AI tools
  • Select pilot teams, define exit criteria and scale what works
  • Keep AI inside existing ceremonies and practices rather than a parallel process
  • Report progress with the Value Analyst and raise blockers with governance and platform

Key artefacts: maturity assessments, adoption roadmap, enablement plan and AI literacy records, pilot reviews.

Disciplines: Skills & Roles · Value & Measurement

AI Governance Officer

Evolves from: risk, compliance, information-security officer

Mission: set AI controls and assure them from the second line, mapping them to regulation and giving independent assurance that they work.

Responsibilities

  • Own the AI usage policy, risk-tier criteria and the inventory of AI tools and services
  • Map controls to the EU AI Act and ISO/IEC 42001 and keep that mapping current
  • Assess third-party AI providers and approve additions to the tool catalogue
  • Test that guardrails, audit trail and monitoring operate as designed
  • Provide second-line oversight of critical-tier work and AI-related incidents

Key artefacts: AI usage policy, policy-as-code rules, AI inventory, regulatory mapping, assurance reports.

Disciplines: Governance & Guardrails

AI Platform Engineer

Evolves from: platform / DevOps engineer

Mission: provide the platform that makes the safe path the easy one: an AI-aware pipeline, approved tools, governed identities and the means to stop AI quickly.

Responsibilities

  • Run the AI-aware pipeline: secret and context checks, provenance, licence and eval gates, tier-driven approvals
  • Provide approved AI tools and integrations through the platform, with data boundaries enforced
  • Manage agents as non-human identities: least privilege, short-lived credentials, recertification
  • Build and test kill switches and action limits for agents and automations
  • Revoke access and archive provenance when AI elements are retired

Key artefacts: pipeline gate configuration, approved tool catalogue, non-human identity register, kill-switch runbooks.

Disciplines: Tooling & Context Engineering · AI-Augmented Delivery

Value Analyst

Evolves from: engineering-metrics or finance analyst

Mission: establish whether AI adoption delivers real value by baselining and measuring outcomes and cost at team and flow level, never for individuals.

Responsibilities

  • Baseline cycle time, rework rate, defect escape rate and review burden before adoption
  • Compare AI-assisted and non-assisted work on the same measures over time
  • Track AI tool and service cost against realised value
  • Keep measurement at team and flow level; refuse per-person productivity tracking
  • Challenge vanity metrics such as lines generated or prompts sent

Key artefacts: metric baselines, value reports, cost and ROI analyses, measurement definitions.

Disciplines: Value & Measurement
